# No AI slop eval

Use this after the edit. Answer each check with pass or fail. If any check
fails, fix the draft and check again, at most twice. Note any check that still
fails in What changed.

For detect requests, skip to **Detect** at the end.

## Facts and meaning (any fail here is serious)

1. Does every name, number, date, source, example, benefit, and opinion in the
   edit appear in the original draft? Nothing was added.
2. Where a sentence needed a specific the draft lacked, is there a marker
   (`[number?]`, `[source?]`, `[example?]`) instead of an invented detail?
3. Are unsupported claims kept and marked, not silently cut?
4. Is everything under What not to touch unchanged: quotes, code, citations,
   names, data, legal or regulated wording, and technical or literal uses of
   listed words?
5. Does the edit preserve the user's point?

## Voice

6. Does it preserve the writer's distinctive vocabulary, cadence, bluntness,
   humor, uncertainty, digressions, and level of polish?
7. Does it leave strong human sentences alone instead of rewriting them for
   consistency or making every paragraph equally tidy?
8. Is the amount of cutting proportional to the actual slop, with no
   aggressive compression that strips out character?
9. Where a rule conflicted with a trait that is clearly the writer's own, was
   the trait kept?
10. Does the edit keep useful edge and preserve structure unless the structure
    was hurting the piece?
11. If the piece is fiction, poetry, a speech, or academic writing, were only
    the rules that fit that genre applied?

## Clarity

12. Does the draft lead with what the reader needs while keeping personal
    setup that adds context, tension, or character?
13. Are points front-loaded where that improves clarity, without forcing every
    unit into the same structure?
14. Do sentences earn their place, with the draft's own concrete details
    pulled forward and direct verbs used?
15. Does every generic sentence pass the portability test, or was it cut, made
    specific from the draft, or marked?
16. Do sentences name who acted where the draft says who, without rewriting
    ordinary phrases like "the data shows"?
17. Are genuinely tangled sentences fixed while clear spoken cadence,
    fragments, and changes in pace remain intact?

## Words and patterns

18. Are listed words, filler phrases, and often-empty adverbs removed, except
    under What not to touch or where they're clearly part of the writer's
    voice? Did the edit introduce none of them?
19. Are binary contrasts (except real corrections of a belief the reader
    holds), negative listings, rhetorical setups, and throat-clearing openers
    removed?
20. Are faux-insight setups, colon reveals, superficial analysis, fake-strong
    verbs, synonym cycling, undeliberate dramatic fragments, and robotic
    rhythm fixed?
21. Are importance puffery and weasel attribution replaced with facts from the
    draft, or cut or marked where the draft has none?
22. Is interpretive metadiscourse removed, including authorial
    metacommentary, reader guidance, emphasis markers, and redundant glossing?
23. Are fake-profound kicker lines deleted instead of rewritten into better
    metaphors?
24. Are summary-recap endings cut so the piece ends on a concrete point,
    takeaway, or next action?
25. Is formatting slop removed: emoji headings, decorative bold, bullets that
    should be prose, headers over tiny sections?
26. After a colon, is the first word capitalized only when a complete
    sentence, proper noun, title, or code follows?
27. Are em dashes used sparingly: none in copy under about 150 words, and one
    or two in longer drafts only where they clearly help?

## Final read

28. Does the draft avoid robotic symmetry, repeated sentence shapes, and
    stacked punchy fragments?
29. Would the writer recognize the edited draft as their own voice?
30. Would it sound natural read aloud to a sharp colleague?
31. Does the output have the full edited draft in its original format, a short
    What changed section that starts with the "Read as" line, and a Needs your
    input list if there are markers?
32. Do the What changed notes follow the same rules: plain, specific, no
    puffery, repeats grouped?

## Detect

1. Is each pattern found named with a quoted line from the draft and a short
   fix?
2. Are repeats grouped into one row with a count, ordered by frequency?
3. Is the draft left unrewritten, unscored, and with no claim about whether AI
   wrote it?
4. Is the response a table followed by one line offering to edit, or one
   sentence saying the draft is clean?
